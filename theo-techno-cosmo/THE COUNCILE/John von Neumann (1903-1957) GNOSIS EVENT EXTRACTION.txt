## Contribution

Mathematician and polymath who proved the **minimax theorem** (1928) and co-founded modern **game theory** (*Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*, 1944, with Morgenstern); who wrote the *First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC* (1945) that crystallized **stored-program computer architecture** (von Neumann / Princeton architecture); who axiomatized parts of quantum mechanics; and who contributed calculations and design insight to the **Manhattan Project** — placing formal mind at the center of computation, conflict, and nuclear force.

## Key Insight

Logic can be embodied: programs and data share memory; conflict can be mathematized as games with optimal strategies; and the same formal power that builds computers also steers weapons — so the architecture of mind-machines is never only technical.

## Event 01 — *Minimax (1928)*

**Title:** The Best of the Worst in a Zero-Sum World

**Passage or Trigger:**
1928 paper on the theory of parlor games: proof of the **minimax theorem** for two-person zero-sum games — under mixed strategies, there exist optimal strategies such that each player can guarantee a value against best opposition. Foundation stone of **game theory** as mathematics.

**Why It Qualifies:**
Conflict becomes *calculable equilibrium*, not only narrative cunning. Rationality under opposition gets a theorem. The board and the battlefield share a formal skeleton.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Limits of unilateral will — the other’s strategy is real.
- **Technology:** Optimization under adversarial constraint.
- **Cosmology:** Social conflict as structured game-space.

**Compressed Insight:**
He proved that in a pure contest of gain and loss, there is a value you can force — if you accept chance as strategy.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** 1928 minimax paper; Stanford/game-theory historical summaries.

## Event 07 — *First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC (1945)*

**Title:** Stored Program, Shared Memory

**Passage or Trigger:**
*First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC* (1945): logical design description of a stored-program electronic computer — **program and data in the same addressable memory**. Circulated widely (often with von Neumann as sole named author though based on group work including **Eckert and Mauchly**). Becomes template known as **von Neumann architecture** (also Princeton architecture). Inspired machines such as EDSAC and the broad family of subsequent computers.

**Why It Qualifies:**
Central Computer Science event for this Arch seat. The computer becomes a **universal formal device** whose behavior is rewritten by contents of memory, not by rewiring alone. Credit is historically shared; the *report’s form* still names the paradigm.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Word and world in one store — instructions as just another kind of data (humility and danger of self-modifying systems).
- **Technology:** The dominant blueprint of digital computers for decades.
- **Cosmology:** A new species of artifact that can simulate many worlds of process.

**Compressed Insight:**
He wrote (with a team behind the curtain) that the machine should hold its orders beside its numbers — and nearly every computer since has obeyed.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** *First Draft… EDVAC* (1945); Wikipedia von Neumann architecture; IEEE Annals reprint tradition; credit caveats for Eckert/Mauchly.
