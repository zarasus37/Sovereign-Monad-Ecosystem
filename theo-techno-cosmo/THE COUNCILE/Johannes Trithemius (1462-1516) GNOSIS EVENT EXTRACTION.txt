I wrote as Abbot of Sponheim (later Würzburg), under Catholic protestation, for princes and studious men. ## Event 01 — *The title of the art*

**Title:** Steganography — intention to the absent

**What I did / What was made:**
I named the work: the sure art of disclosing the mind’s intention to those who are not present, through secret writing.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> STEGANOGRAPHY:  
> That is:  
> THE SURE ART  
> OF DISCLOSING  
> THE INTENTION OF  
> ONE’S MIND TO THOSE WHO ARE ABSENT  
> THROUGH SECRET WRITING;  
> BY  
> THAT MOST REVEREND AND FAMOUS MAN,  
> Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, &  
> Most Perfect Master of Natural  
> Magic.  
> PREFIXED TO THIS WORK IS ITS KEY, OR  
> a true introduction, composed by the Author himself;  
> HERETOFORE MUCH DESIRED BY MANY,  
> though seen by very few…

— Johannes Trithemius, *Steganographia*, title apparatus as in Frankfurt 1606 print line; English presentation: trithemius.com/steganographia-english (authorial title block). Latin title tradition: *Steganographia*.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
The problem is not “magic vs science.” The problem is how one mind’s intention reaches another who is **absent** — securely, without detection.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Communication redefined as secret disclosure of intention across absence.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Natural magic under abbatial and Christian frame of the title.
- **Technology:** Secret writing as the instrument of remote intention.
- **Cosmology:** Absence as the field the art must cross.

**Compressed Insight:**
I teach how to disclose intention to those who are absent — through secret writing.

**Confidence:** 0.97  
**Sources:** *Steganographia* title apparatus continuous.

## Event 02 — *For Prince Philip — door to hidden secrets*

**Title:** Ancient sages conceal; I unlock a door

**What I did / What was made:**
I dedicated Book I to Philip, Count Palatine, and stated why the sages hide mysteries — and what I have unlocked for secure remote thought.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> It is the opinion of the learned that whenever the ancient sages, whom in the Greek language we call Philosophers, discovered any secret of art or nature, they concealed it through various modes and figures lest the knowledge of it should fall into the hands of the wicked. That even Moses, the famed leader of the Israelite race, in his description of the creation of Heaven and Earth, concealed with simple words the ineffable arcana of these mysteries, all the scholars of the Jews confirm. Amongst our own, the divine and learned Jerome affirms that there are almost as many mysteries lying hidden in the Apocalypse of John as there are words… And even though I myself, on account of the slowness of my abilities, am not able to imitate fully these studious lovers of Wisdom, nevertheless I admire them and, insofar as I am able, do not omit to read them with diligence. For when I consider the grand things that these men beyond all others have conceived through their studies, I begin to chafe with a certain vehemence until I grow warm and I force myself toward some emulation of my predecessors… For even though I am neither so learned nor industrious as to dare profess that I have grasped fully the method of the ancient sages for concealing mysteries, nevertheless, I have uncovered certain ways, both numerous and varied, that are not (so it seems to me) to be entirely spurned whereby I can intimate my most secret thoughts to another who knows this art, however far away I wish, securely and free from the deceit, suspicion, or detection by anyone, using writing or openly through messengers. The new discovery of this most secret art I have committed to writing at the insistence of the Most Serene Prince, Lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine… But lest this great secret should reach the ears of the ignorant masses or wicked men, I judged it to be not the last accounting of my duty to envelope it in mysteries since it teaches the unknowing to understand mysteries. And I did this in such a way that no one from the number of the ignorant (no one unless he is most studious) will by his own power be able to penetrate completely the arcana of this new science to the full extent of our meaning — unless, that is, through its direct reception from a teacher, what the Hebrews call *Cabala* which is preeminent among the occult mysteries.

— Trithemius, *Steganographia* Book I, dedication / prologue continuous; English: trithemius.com (Book I opening to Philip). Latin print 1606 / IA BSB 1608.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
I stand in the line of those who hide by figure. My contribution is practical: secret thoughts to one who shares the art, at any distance, free of detection — enveloped so the thick-headed cannot break it without a teacher.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Concealment as duty of wisdom; Cabala as transmission condition; remote secure thought.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Moses, Apocalypse, Cabala as precedents of hidden word.
- **Technology:** Steganography as method of secure remote intention.
- **Cosmology:** Distance not absolute barrier when art is shared.

**Compressed Insight:**
I intimate my most secret thoughts however far away — securely — and I envelope the art so the ignorant cannot seize it.

**Confidence:** 0.97  
**Sources:** *Steganographia* I prologue continuous (English vehicle of Latin).

## Event 03 — *Protestation before Almighty God*

**Title:** Sworn innocence of the art

**What I did / What was made:**
I placed a solemn oath at the head of the work: no pact with demons; Catholic and monastic; judge the art after understanding it.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> Therefore I say and openly before Almighty God, before whom absolutely nothing can be hidden, and openly before Jesus Christ, his only begotten son who will judge the living and the dead, in truth I say, I swear and I protest: Each and everything that I have said in this work, or am about to say, and all the properties, methods, figures, operations, traditions, conventions, formations, inventions, foundations, modifications and alterations of this art or science and everything which pertains to its contemplation, contrivance, acquisition, operation and practice, whether in part or in whole, and everything which is contained within this volume of ours, relies upon true, catholic and natural principles and each and everything is done in accordance with God in good conscience and without injury to the Christian faith, preserving the integrity of Ecclesiastic tradition, without any superstition, without idolatry, without any pact with malign spirits whether explicit or implicit, without any incense-burning, adoration, veneration, worship, sacrifice or offerings to demons and free from all fault and sin whether venial or mortal, and that each and everything is done in accordance with truth, rectitude, sincerity and purity since the knowledge of this invention and its application to good ends is not indecent for a man who is wise and a good and faithful Christian. For I am also a Christian and voluntarily bound to monastic practice, and I desire to live and abide not otherwise than as befits a true Christian and professed monk under the rule of the divine father Benedict… Far be it from me either to learn or teach anything that is contrary to Christian faith and purity, offensive to holy morals or adverse to our professed rule. I fear God and I have sworn myself to his worship from which I shall never be separated in life and, as I trust, in death.

> This protestation of mine I have placed at the beginning of this whole work not without reason because I know there will be many bent on injury who, since they cannot understand what we have written, will dismiss our good and holy studies as evil arts and superstitious contrivances… Learn first this art, then judge. But if you cannot understand it, blame not it (since it is good) but your dull abilities.

Latin (print / OCR line of the oath opening):

> …iuro & protestor: Omnia & singula, quae in hoc opere dixi, vel dicturus sum…

— Trithemius, *Steganographia* Book I, “Protestation or sworn oath of the Author”; English continuous: trithemius.com; Latin fragment: IA *Steganographia* BSB 1608 OCR.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
I know they will call me magus and necromancer. I answer under oath before God and Christ: catholic, natural, no pact — learn before you sentence.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Self-implication of author under church and state suspicion; truth of the art claimed under God.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Oath before God and Christ; Benedictine rule.
- **Technology:** Art claimed as natural/catholic principles of operation.
- **Cosmology:** Judgment of living and dead frames the claim of purity.

**Compressed Insight:**
I swear: no pact with malign spirits — learn the art before you judge it.

**Confidence:** 0.98  
**Sources:** *Steganographia* protestation continuous English + Latin oath incipit.

## Event 04 — *Good and bad use of the same art*

**Title:** Public trust under secret writing

**What I did / What was made:**
I warned that the same discovery that serves good men can, in twisted hands, unsettle the whole order of the State and poison every letter with suspicion.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> Nor is it the case that the knowledge of this secret art, divulged to the wicked and perverse, would harm the State less than it would benefit were it divulged to good men. For just as good men and those who have a zeal for virtue put every discovery to a good and communal use, thus do evil and perverse men hunt opportunities to corrupt not only what was established for evil, but also what had been established as good and sacred… For just as it offers itself as an instrument for good in the hands of good and holy men, so does it present itself to the impious and the perverted as an aid to their crimes… For though this knowledge is good in and of itself and quite useful to the State, nevertheless if it reached the attention of twisted men (God forbid), over time the whole order of the State would become disturbed, and not in a small way. Public trust would be in peril. Every letter, document and writ, and even people’s very conversations would fall under the gravest suspicion. No one without trepidation would believe anything written, however holy and honest… People would become fearful of everyone and in general suspicious of their friends no less than their enemies.

— Trithemius, *Steganographia* Book I prologue continuous (good/evil use; State; public trust); English: trithemius.com.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
The art is dual-use. I will not pretend holiness of medium guarantees holiness of operator. Trust itself is at stake.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Cryptography as civil theology of trust — not only private cleverness.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Good and holy vs impious use under God forbid.
- **Technology:** Dual-use secret writing; covertext that looks chaste.
- **Cosmology:** Social order as fragile under perfect concealment.

**Compressed Insight:**
If twisted men get this art, public trust falls — every holy-looking letter becomes suspect.

**Confidence:** 0.97  
**Sources:** *Steganographia* I prologue continuous.

## Event 05 — *Pamersiel — first letters of every word*

**Title:** Cover prayer, hidden intention

**What I did / What was made:**
I taught Book I ch. 1 under spirit-prince Pamersiel: write an open narration anyone can read; the secret rides the first letters; the receiver uses the matching conjuration.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

Operational key (German gloss embedded in the tradition of the chapter):

> nym di ersten bugstaben de omni uerbo  
> \[take the first letters of every word\]

And:

> sum taly cautala it pryme lytore cuiuslybet diccionys secretam intencionem tuam reddant legenty  
> \[take care such that the first letters of each word return to the reader your secret meaning\]

Method in his English-vehicle continuous instruction:

> Let him prepare with an invocation of the divine name, in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, the paper on which he will write. Next let him write on it any simple and open narration he wants such that anyone who reads it can understand it, whether in Latin, his native tongue, or any other language… Write a simple and open narration of this sort, or any other that you want, which anyone can read and understand and which contains nothing secret and which you do not fear coming to the notice of anyone… Once the narration is written, express directly to the spirits the secret that you wish someone who is not present to know.

Example covertext opens as open prayer (Latin) while secret intention is separately stated in the chapter apparatus (e.g. war-order / vassal plaintexts in the print tradition).

— Trithemius, *Steganographia* Book I, Chapter 1 (Pamersiel); English continuous + embedded German/Latin keys: trithemius.com “1.1 Pamersiel.” **Secondary note (not his sentence):** modern cryptanalysis reads the “conjurations” and covertexts as steganographic first-letter ciphers — consistent with the keys he embeds (*nym di ersten bugstaben*).

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
The letter everyone may read is the cover. The first letters carry intention to the one who shares the art. Spirit-names and directions are the routing table of the same machine.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Unity of covertext, key, and “spirit” apparatus in one operational chapter.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Invocation of Trinity; spirit-prince as named agency.
- **Technology:** First-letter steganography under open narration.
- **Cosmology:** East-facing sectors / spirit compass as spatial protocol.

**Compressed Insight:**
Take the first letters of every word — the open prayer returns secret intention to the reader who knows.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** *Steganographia* I.1 continuous.

## Event 06 — *Book III — Menastor and thought without messenger*

**Title:** Mind known afar without words, books, or courier

**What I did / What was made:**
I opened Book III: after two books of the art, still more wondrous — thoughts known to a friend however far, in twenty-four hours, without words, books, or messenger — from the ancient Menastor, seven planets, seven angels, twenty-one spirits.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> AFTER, with the aid of our Lord Jesus Christ, the savior of faithful souls, we completed the first two books of our art, in which we described, as it were, our Steganography, it seems proper that we add to those this third book, and unto those wondrous contrivances join things even more wondrous. I found, in a certain book by a certain ancient Philosopher, who is called Menastor, that it is possible that, using a certain art, we can make the thoughts of our mind known to a friend, however far away, in 24 hours, without words, without books, and without a messenger, most perfectly, amply, and secretly. And as I was drawn most vehemently by the desire of knowing this, I began to want to test the truth of these assertions, and after much toil at last I found this knowledge to be vindicated by experiment. The secret of this sort of art is very deep, and wrapped up in obscurities so great, that it is not accessible to anyone easily, for Menastor enveloped this mystery exceedingly obscurely and with few words… But I, fearing lest a work so wondrous on account of its excessive obscurity would be held cheap by the ignorant, or even burned in the fire, and whereas I, for a long time now, have examined it with many tests, thus I wanted to commit it into writing that is more open, so that, with God’s help, it may, to some extent, also become accessible to learned men and those most studious of magical things, while, however, remaining at all times hidden from the ignorant “turnip eaters” and to no extent understood by their obtuse intellects.
>
> Quoth Menastor: There are seven planets, over which seven Angels preside, and subordinate to these are 21 spirits through whom the secrets are conveyed. We shall set forth their names in a table.

— Trithemius, *Steganographia* Book III, Preface continuous; English: trithemius.com (Book III opening). **Secondary note:** Book III formulae also decrypt as further cryptography (late 20th c. scholarship); his **sentences** remain the voice of experiment, Menastor, and the sevenfold angel table.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
I push past writing that still looks like writing. I claim experiment: mind to friend, far, in a day — without courier — under seven planetary angels.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Limit-case of remote mind; angelic table as channel architecture.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Christ as aid; seven Angels over planets.
- **Technology:** Art validated by experiment; obscurity as access control.
- **Cosmology:** Planetary–angelic network for secret conveyance.

**Compressed Insight:**
Thoughts of the mind known to a friend however far — without words, books, or messenger — under seven angels of the planets.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** *Steganographia* III preface continuous.

## Event 07 — *Seven secondary intelligences*

**Title:** Orifiel and the governors after God

**What I did / What was made:**
I wrote *De septem secundeis*: seven secondary causes — intelligences or spirits moving the orbs after God — beginning with Orifiel of Saturn, to whom God committed government of the world from creation’s beginning.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

Latin:

> Primus Angelus, sive Spiritus Saturni vocatur **Orifiel**, cui Deus mundum a principio creationis gubernandum commisit…

English (Lilly 1647 vehicle of Trithemius):

> The first Angell or Spirit of *Saturn* is called *Orifiel*, to whom God committed the government of the World from the beginning of the Creation…

(The work continues through the seven planetary angels and successive ages of their government of world history — Trithemius’s chronological angelology.)

— Johannes Trithemius, *De septem secundeis, id est intelligentiis sive spiritibus orbes post Deum moventibus* (1508; printed editions 1522 ff.). Latin continuous incipit as in standard editions (e.g. esotericarchives presentation); English: William Lilly, *The Worlds Catastrophe* (London, 1647), Englishing of *De Septem Secundeis*.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
After God, secondary intelligences move the orbs. History is not only human will; it is also appointed governorship under planetary spirits.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Cosmic time as angelic administration; same spirit-network language as Steganographia tables.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** God commits government; secondaries after God.
- **Technology:** Chronologia as ordered table of ages and governors.
- **Cosmology:** Orbs moved by intelligences; world-history under cycle.

**Compressed Insight:**
Orifiel first — to whom God committed the world’s government from the beginning.

**Confidence:** 0.95  
**Sources:** *De septem secundeis* Latin + Lilly English continuous opening.

## Event 08 — *Chaos of infinite depth*

**Title:** Always less learned than what remains unknown

**What I did / What was made:**
I described Steganography as a chaos no one finishes — infinite ways of secret writing, student exceeding teacher under Cabalistic transmission.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> Nor could anyone, even if he lived a thousand years with his teacher, become so learned and expert in this science that there would not still remain infinite ways of writing secretly, covertly and securely with this same art… For just as the good and bad aerial spirits created by God on high to assist and profit us (through an understanding of which, all the secrets of this art are revealed) are infinite beyond number and utterly incomprehensible, so likewise all the ways, methods, distinctions, characteristics and operations of this our art which we call Steganography… cannot be accessed in full. This science is a chaos of infinite depth which no one can comprehend completely. For however expert and adept you may be in this art, you will always have learned less than what you do not know. This most deep and secret art has the property that it easily makes the student incomparably more learned than his teacher… provided that he is disposed by nature to advance and that he is diligent in what he has learned in the Cabalistic tradition.

— Trithemius, *Steganographia* Book I prologue continuous; English: trithemius.com.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
I do not close the system. The art is infinite like the spirits that assist it — the student may outrun the teacher, and still stand before unexplored depth.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Epistemic humility inside a complete-looking apparatus.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Aerial spirits created by God to assist and profit.
- **Technology:** Infinite methods under one art-name.
- **Cosmology:** Chaos of depth as structure of the knowable secret.

**Compressed Insight:**
Steganography is a chaos of infinite depth — you always know less than what remains.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** *Steganographia* I prologue continuous.
