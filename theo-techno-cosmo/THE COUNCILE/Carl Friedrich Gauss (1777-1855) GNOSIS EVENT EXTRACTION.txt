## Contribution

I founded **Higher Arithmetic** as a systematic science in *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* (1801); determined orbits from any number of observations by the **most probable system** in which the **sum of the squares of the differences** is a minimum (*Theoria motus*, 1809); and proved that the **measure of curvature** of a surface is **unchanged** when the surface is developed upon any other (*Disquisitiones generales circa superficies curvas*, 1827) — opening geometry of flexible, non-extensible surfaces.

## Key Insight

Integers are the proper object of Higher Arithmetic, not mere calculation; when observations over-determine an orbit, probability leads to minimizing the sum of squared residuals; and curvature measured from the surface’s own linear element survives bending — so number, error, and space each have intrinsic law.

## Event 01 — *Higher Arithmetic*

**Title:** Inquiries that concern integers

**What I did / What was made:**
I defined the object of the *Disquisitiones*: Higher Arithmetic as general inquiry concerning integers, distinct from elementary calculating and from Diophantine dexterity alone.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> The inquiries which this volume will investigate pertain to that part of Mathematics which concerns itself with integers. I will rarely refer to fractions and never to surds. The Analysis which is called indeterminate or Diophantine and which discusses the manner of selecting from an infinite set of solutions for an indeterminate problem those that are integral or at least rational (and especially with the added condition that they be positive) is not the discipline to which I refer but rather a special part of it, just as the art of reducing and solving equations (Algebra) is a special part of universal Analysis. And as we include under the heading ANALYSIS all discussion that involves quantity, so integers (and fractions in so far as they are determined by integers) constitute the proper object of ARITHMETIC. However what is commonly called Arithmetic hardly extends beyond the art of enumerating and calculating (i.e. expressing numbers by suitable symbols, for example by a decimal representation, and carrying out arithmetic operations). It often includes some subjects which certainly do not pertain to Arithmetic (like the theory of logarithms) and others which are common to all quantities. As a result it seems proper to call this subject Elementary Arithmetic and to distinguish from it Higher Arithmetic which properly includes more general inquiries concerning integers. We consider only Higher Arithmetic in the present volume.
>
> Included under the heading “Higher Arithmetic” are those topics which Euclid treated with elegance and rigor in Book VII ff., and they can be considered an introduction to this science. The celebrated work of Diophantus… seems to fit into that epoch in the history of Mathematics when scientists were more concerned with creating a characteristic art and a formal Algebraic structure than with attempts to enrich Higher Arithmetic with new discoveries. The really profound discoveries are due to more recent authors like those men of immortal glory P de Fermat, L Euler, L Lagrange, A M Legendre (and a few others). They opened the door to what is penetrable in this divine science and enriched it with enormous wealth.

— Carl Friedrich Gauss, *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* (1801), Author’s Preface continuous. English after Arthur A. Clarke (Yale 1966), MacTutor continuous carrier of Dedication/Preface.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Not counting tricks. Integers as proper object. Euclid opens; Diophantus shows skill; Fermat–Euler–Lagrange–Legendre open the divine science I continue.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Number theory named and ranked as Higher Arithmetic — a kingdom, not a bag of problems.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Higher Arithmetic as “divine science” entered through predecessors’ door.
- **Technology:** Distinction elementary vs higher — method of inquiry, not only calculation.
- **Cosmology:** Integers as structured domain of pure form.

**Compressed Insight:**
The inquiries concern integers — Higher Arithmetic, not the mere art of enumerating and calculating.

**Confidence:** 0.98  
**Sources:** *Disquisitiones* Preface continuous (Clarke English).

## Event 02 — *I was unaware of the more recent discoveries*

**Title:** Chance truth and successive results

**What I did / What was made:**
I narrated how an extraordinary arithmetic truth in 1795 pulled me into the field before I knew the modern literature — then walking in their footsteps I extended Arithmetic further.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> The purpose of this volume whose publication I promised five years ago is to present my investigations into the field of Higher Arithmetic. Lest anyone be surprised that the contents here go back over many first principles and that many results had been given energetic attention by other authors, I must explain to the reader that when I first turned to this type of inquiry in the beginning of 1795 I was unaware of the more recent discoveries in the field and was without the means of discovering them. What happened was this. Engaged in other work I chanced on an extraordinary arithmetic truth (if I am not mistaken, it was the theorem of art. 108 [−1 is a quadratic residue of all numbers of the form 4*n*+1 and a non-residue of all numbers of the form 4*n*+3]). Since I considered it so beautiful in itself and since I suspected its connection with even more profound results, I concentrated on it all my efforts in order to understand the principles on which it depended and to obtain a rigorous proof. When I succeeded in this I was so attracted by these questions that I could not let them be. Thus as one result led to another I had completed most of what is presented in the first four sections of this work before I came into contact with similar works of other geometers. Only while studying the writings of these men of genius did I recognize that the greater part of my meditations had been spent on subjects already well developed. But this only increased my interest, and walking in their footsteps I attempted to extend Arithmetic further. Some of these results are embodied in Sections V, VI, and VII.
>
> …
>
> In several difficult discussions I have used synthetic proofs and have suppressed the analysis which led to the results. This was necessitated by brevity, a consideration that must be consulted as much as possible.
>
> …
>
> These are the things I wanted to warn the reader about. It is not my place to judge the work itself. My greatest hope is that it pleases those who have at heart the development of science and that it proposes solutions that they have been looking for or at least opens the way for new investigations.

— Gauss, *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*, Author’s Preface continuous (Clarke English).

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Beauty of one truth → proof → chain of results → then the literature. Independent rediscovery humbled and accelerated me. Synthetic proofs for brevity.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Method of discovery told honestly: attraction, rigor, then extension beyond predecessors.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Beauty as lure toward principles and proof.
- **Technology:** Synthetic presentation; analysis suppressed for space.
- **Cosmology:** One residue theorem opening a connected field of integer law.

**Compressed Insight:**
I chanced on an extraordinary arithmetic truth — beautiful, connected to deeper results — and could not let the questions be.

**Confidence:** 0.98  
**Sources:** *Disquisitiones* Preface continuous.

## Event 03 — *More data than required*

**Title:** Errors and the calculus of probabilities

**What I did / What was made:**
In determining orbits I stated why many observations must be used: all are liable to error; small errors are more common; an orbit that fits six data exactly but ignores the rest is less probable.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> … we should not insure the greatest accuracy, if we were to select three or four positions only for the determination of the orbit, and neglect all the rest. But in such a case, if it is proposed to aim at the greatest precision, we shall take care to collect and employ the greatest possible number of accurate places. Then, of course, more data will exist than are required for the determination of the unknown quantities: but all these data will be liable to errors, however small, so that it will generally be impossible to satisfy all perfectly. Now as no reason exists, why, from among those data, we should consider any six as absolutely exact, but since we must assume, rather, upon the principles of probability, that greater or less errors are equally possible in all, promiscuously; since, moreover, generally speaking, small errors oftener occur than large ones; it is evident, that an orbit which, while it satisfies precisely the six data, deviates more or less from the others, must be regarded as less consistent with the principles of the calculus of probabilities, than one which, at the same time that it differs a little from those six data, presents so much the better an agreement with the rest. The investigation of an orbit having, strictly speaking, the maximum probability, will depend upon a knowledge of the law according to which the probability of errors decreases as the errors increase in magnitude: but that depends upon so many vague and doubtful considerations — physiological included — which cannot be subjected to calculation, that it is scarcely, and indeed less than scarcely, possible to assign properly a law of this kind in any case of practical astronomy. Nevertheless, an investigation of the connection between this law and the most probable orbit, which we will undertake in its utmost generality, is not to be regarded as by any means a barren speculation.

— Gauss, *Theory of the Motion of the Heavenly Bodies Moving about the Sun in Conic Sections* (Davis English of *Theoria motus*, 1809/1857), Book II continuous (before art. 175).

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Use all accurate places. No six are sacred. Probability prefers small shared disagreement over perfect fit to a few and large misses of the rest.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Observational cosmos under error law — method for Ceres-class problems and all over-determined measurement.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Humility before error — no absolute exactness of finite observation.
- **Technology:** Collect greatest number of accurate places; probability calculus as instrument.
- **Cosmology:** Orbit as most probable under error, not unique geometric fiction.

**Compressed Insight:**
More data than unknowns — all liable to error; the orbit of maximum probability must agree with the whole, not six alone.

**Confidence:** 0.99  
**Sources:** *Theoria motus* Davis continuous.

## Event 04 — *Sum of the squares a minimum*

**Title:** Most probable system of values

**What I did / What was made:**
From the error law implied by the arithmetical mean axiom I derived: the most probable unknowns minimize the sum of squares of observed-minus-computed differences — an axiom for mathematics applied to natural philosophy.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> Now it readily follows from this, that the most probable system of values of the quantities *p*, *q*, *r*, *s*, etc. is that in which Ω acquires the maximum value…
>
> … It has been customary certainly to regard as an axiom the hypothesis that if any quantity has been determined by several direct observations, made under the same circumstances and with equal care, the arithmetical mean of the observed values affords the most probable value, if not rigorously, yet very nearly at least, so that it is always most safe to adhere to it. …
>
> …
>
> We will now develop the conclusions which follow from this law. It is evident, in order that the product may become a maximum, that the sum
>
> *vv* + *v′v′* + *v″v″* + etc.,
>
> must become a minimum. Therefore, that will be the most probable system of values of the unknown quantities *p*, *q*, *r*, *s*, etc., in which the sum of the squares of the differences between the observed and computed values of the functions *V*, *V′*, *V″*, etc. is a minimum, if the same degree of accuracy is to be presumed in all the observations. This principle, which promises to be of most frequent use in all applications of the mathematics to natural philosophy, must, everywhere, be considered an axiom with the same propriety as the arithmetical mean of several observed values of the same quantity is adopted as the most probable value.
>
> This principle can be extended without difficulty to observations of unequal accuracy. … the most probable system of values of the quantities *p*, *q*, *r*, *s*, etc., will be that in which the sum of *hhvv* + *h′h′v′v′* + *h″h″v″v″* + etc., that is, in which the sum of the squares of the differences between the actually observed and computed values multiplied by numbers that measure the degree of precision, is a minimum.

— Gauss, *Theoria motus* (Davis English), arts. 177–179 continuous (probability product → mean axiom → least squares). Priority note: Legendre published a least-squares method 1805; Gauss here grounds the principle in probability.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Maximize joint probability of the errors → minimize sum of squares. Same dignity as taking the arithmetic mean. Weight by precision when observations are unequal.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Error law becomes operational axiom for all applied mathematics of observation.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Probability as lawful structure of imperfect human measure.
- **Technology:** Linear algorithm for unknowns when functions are linear; weights *h*.
- **Cosmology:** Heavenly bodies’ orbits recovered under Gaussian error.

**Compressed Insight:**
The most probable system is that in which the sum of the squares of the differences between observed and computed values is a minimum.

**Confidence:** 0.99  
**Sources:** *Theoria motus* Davis arts. 177–179 continuous.

## Event 05 — *Sphere of unit radius*

**Title:** Directions represented on the auxiliary sphere

**What I did / What was made:**
I opened the general investigations of curved surfaces by representing directions of lines by points on a unit sphere — the auxiliary for orientation and curvature.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> Investigations, in which the directions of various straight lines in space are to be considered, attain a high degree of clearness and simplicity if we employ, as an auxiliary, a sphere of unit radius described about an arbitrary centre, and suppose the different points of the sphere to represent the directions of straight lines parallel to the radii ending at these points. As the position of every point in space is determined by three coordinates, that is to say, the distances of the point from three mutually perpendicular fixed planes, it is necessary to consider, first of all, the directions of the axes perpendicular to these planes. The points on the sphere, which represent these directions, we shall denote by (1), (2), (3). The distance of any one of these points from either of the other two will be a quadrant; and we shall suppose that the directions of the axes are those in which the corresponding coordinates increase.
>
> It will be advantageous to bring together here some propositions which are frequently used in questions of this kind.
>
> I. The angle between two intersecting straight lines is measured by the arc between the points on the sphere which correspond to the directions of the lines.
>
> II. The orientation of any plane whatever can be represented by the great circle on the sphere, the plane of which is parallel to the given plane.
>
> III. The angle between two planes is equal to the spherical angle between the great circles representing them, and, consequently, is also measured by the arc intercepted between the poles of these great circles. And, in like manner, the angle of inclination of a straight line to a plane is measured by the arc drawn from the point which corresponds to the direction of the line, perpendicular to the great circle which represents the orientation of the plane.

— Gauss, *General Investigations of Curved Surfaces* (1827), §§1–2 continuous. English Morehead & Hiltebeitel (Princeton 1902); Gutenberg #36856.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Directions become points on a unit sphere. Angles of lines and planes become spherical arcs. Geometry of orientation clarified before curvature.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Spherical representation as instrument for surface theory (and geodesy).

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Order of direction made simple by a pure auxiliary sphere.
- **Technology:** Unit sphere as computational and conceptual instrument.
- **Cosmology:** Space’s orientations mapped to a closed finite figure.

**Compressed Insight:**
Directions of lines attain clearness if represented by points on a sphere of unit radius.

**Confidence:** 0.99  
**Sources:** Surfaces 1827 §§1–2 continuous.

## Event 06 — *Remarkable Theorem*

**Title:** Measure of curvature unchanged under development

**What I did / What was made:**
I proved that if a curved surface is developed upon any other surface, the measure of curvature at each point remains unchanged — the *theorema egregium* in continuous statement.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> Suppose that our surface can be developed upon another surface, curved or plane, so that to each point of the former surface, determined by the coordinates *x*, *y*, *z*, will correspond a definite point of the latter surface, whose coordinates are *x′*, *y′*, *z′*. Evidently *x′*, *y′*, *z′* can also be regarded as functions of the indeterminates *p*, *q*, and therefore for the element √(*dx′*² + *dy′*² + *dz′*²) we shall have an expression of the form √(*E′ dp*² + 2*F′ dp·dq* + *G′ dq*²), where *E′*, *F′*, *G′* also denote functions of *p*, *q*. But from the very notion of the development of one surface upon another it is clear that the elements corresponding to one another on the two surfaces are necessarily equal. Therefore we shall have identically
>
> *E* = *E′*, *F* = *F′*, *G* = *G′*.
>
> Thus the formula of the preceding article leads of itself to the remarkable Theorem. If a curved surface is developed upon any other surface whatever, the measure of curvature in each point remains unchanged.
>
> Also it is evident that any finite part whatever of the curved surface will retain the same integral curvature after development upon another surface.
>
> Surfaces developable upon a plane constitute the particular case to which geometers have heretofore restricted their attention. Our theory shows at once that the measure of curvature at every point of such surfaces is equal to zero.

— Gauss, *General Investigations of Curved Surfaces* (1827), continuous (development / remarkable Theorem). Morehead & Hiltebeitel English.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Equal corresponding linear elements force equal *E*, *F*, *G*. Therefore curvature, built from those, cannot change under pure bending. Planes and cylinders share zero measure of curvature.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Intrinsic geometry: curvature is of the surface itself, not of its embedding alone.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Invariant under transformation of form — essence retained.
- **Technology:** Criterion for developable surfaces; map/survey implications.
- **Cosmology:** Space of the surface has its own measure independent of ambient bending.

**Compressed Insight:**
If a curved surface is developed upon any other surface whatever, the measure of curvature in each point remains unchanged.

**Confidence:** 0.99  
**Sources:** Surfaces 1827 continuous (remarkable Theorem).

## Event 07 — *Flexible, though not extensible*

**Title:** New and fertile field

**What I did / What was made:**
I distinguished properties that depend on the form into which a surface is bent from absolute properties that remain invariable — measure of curvature, shortest lines — and opened that study.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> What we have explained in the preceding article is connected with a particular method of studying surfaces, a very worthy method which may be thoroughly developed by geometers. When a surface is regarded, not as the boundary of a solid, but as a flexible, though not extensible solid, one dimension of which is supposed to vanish, then the properties of the surface depend in part upon the form to which we can suppose it reduced, and in part are absolute and remain invariable, whatever may be the form into which the surface is bent. To these latter properties, the study of which opens to geometry a new and fertile field, belong the measure of curvature and the integral curvature, in the sense which we have given to these expressions. To these belong also the theory of shortest lines, and a great part of what we reserve to be treated later. From this point of view, a plane surface and a surface developable on a plane, e.g., cylindrical surfaces, conical surfaces, etc., are to be regarded as essentially identical; and the generic method of defining in a general manner the nature of the surfaces thus considered is always based upon the formula √(*E dp*² + 2*F dp·dq* + *G dq*²), which connects the linear element with the two indeterminates *p*, *q*.

— Gauss, *General Investigations of Curved Surfaces* (1827), continuous (flexible surface program). Morehead & Hiltebeitel English.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Treat the surface as a thin flexible sheet that does not stretch. Absolute properties survive bending. Plane and cylinder are the same kind. The linear element formula is the definition of the surface’s intrinsic nature.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Birth statement of intrinsic differential geometry.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Absolute vs form-dependent — what remains through change of figure.
- **Technology:** Surveyor’s surface as flexible sheet; geodesics as shortest lines.
- **Cosmology:** Geometry generated from the linear element alone.

**Compressed Insight:**
A surface as flexible though not extensible — absolute properties open to geometry a new and fertile field.

**Confidence:** 0.99  
**Sources:** Surfaces 1827 continuous.

## Event 08 — *Geometry not with arithmetic a priori*

**Title:** Necessity of our geometry unproved

**What I did / What was made:**
In correspondence I stated that the necessity of Euclidean geometry cannot be demonstrated for human reason, and that geometry ranks with mechanics, not with purely a priori arithmetic.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> I am coming more and more to the conviction that the necessity of our geometry cannot be demonstrated, at least neither by, nor for, the human intellect… geometry should be ranked, not with arithmetic, which is purely aprioristic, but with mechanics.

— Carl Friedrich Gauss, letter (documented continuous English of his German; commonly cited from correspondence with Olbers, 28 April 1817; related formulations to Taurinus 1824). MacTutor continuous carrier of the conviction.  
*Hold note:* Full German letter texts vary by correspondent; this is the locked continuous English sense-unit of his non-Euclidean conviction. Public systematic non-Euclidean geometry published by Bolyai and Lobachevsky — not claimed here as his sole published treatise.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Arithmetic is pure mind-product. Space may have a reality we cannot prescribe a priori. Geometry belongs with empirical-mechanical rank until proof of necessity appears — and I doubt such proof for us.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Space’s geometry freed from forced Euclidean a priori — privately held, publicly seeded by others.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Humility of the intellect before space’s necessity.
- **Technology:** Survey and measurement as possible tests of geometry’s rank with mechanics.
- **Cosmology:** Physical space not assumed Euclidean by pure reason alone.

**Compressed Insight:**
The necessity of our geometry cannot be demonstrated for the human intellect — geometry with mechanics, not with a priori arithmetic.

**Confidence:** 0.92  
**Sources:** Gauss letter continuous English (Olbers/Taurinus tradition); Bolyai–Lobachevsky public priority noted.
