## Contribution

I made an **Art** — figures, letters for divine dignities and relations, combinatorial tables — so that truth about God and creation might be found by **method**. I wrote it in Catalan and Latin (*Llibre de contemplació*, the *Ars* cycle, *Libre del gentil*, *Blanquerna* with *Libre d’amic e amat*, *Arbor scientiae*, *Ars brevis*, *Ars generalis ultima*). I lived so the Art would not stay paper: language, mission, fear of the body, and death itself had to answer it.

## Key Insight

Constraint generates possibility: when God’s attributes and relations are fixed as principles and set to combine under rule, the mind invents true questions and true answers without losing the unity of the whole.

## Event 01 — *The song interrupted*

**Title:** Five times the Crucified broke the canticle

**What I did / What was made:**
I sat to compose a worldly love-song in the vulgar tongue when the vision of Christ on the Cross cut the act of writing — five times — until conscience fixed three intentions.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> Ramon, while still a young man and Seneschal to the King of Majorca, was very given to composing worthless songs and poems and to doing other licentious things. One night he was sitting beside his bed, about to compose and write in his vulgar tongue a song to a lady whom he loved with a foolish love; and as he began to write this song, he looked to his right and saw our Lord Jesus Christ on the Cross, as if suspended in mid-air.

— *Vita coaetanea* (conversion), English: Bonner, cited in standard editions / Wikipedia from Bonner 1985, pp. 10–11; also Bonner, *A Contemporary Life* (Tamesis, 2010).

Stanford Encyclopedia (Priani) adds the conscience-line from *De vita* I–4 (Bonner tr.):

> his conscience told him that they (the apparitions) could only mean that he should abandon the world at once and from then on dedicate himself totally to the service of our Lord Jesus Christ.

The vision came five times and fixed three intentions: to give up the soul for God’s love and honor; to convert the Saracens; to write the best book against the errors of the unbelievers.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
My craft of desire was not neutral. The song and the Cross could not share one unexamined love. I was writing on the wrong axis.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Object of desire collapses: lady of the song / Christ of the vision.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** God as interrupting presence.
- **Technology:** Vision overrides the production of the song.
- **Cosmology:** Desire and sacrifice share one world.

**Compressed Insight:**
I knew my language of love was already aimed at a Beloved who would cost me the life that wrote the song.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** *Vita coaetanea* / Bonner English; SEP “Ramon Llull” §1.1.

## Event 02 — *Autobiographical first person of the five visions*

**Title:** “It pleased Jesus Christ… five times as if crucified”

**What I did / What was made:**
I also left a first-person line of the five presentations of Christ crucified — so the seat may speak not only through the third-person *Vita* but through my own “I.”

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> When I was grown and knew the world and its vanities, I began to do evil and entered on sin. Forgetting the true God I went after carnal things. But it pleased Jesus Christ in His great pity to present Himself to me five times as if crucified, that I might remember Him and set my love on Him, doing what I could that He might be known through all the world and the truth be taught concerning the great Trinity and the Incarnation. And thus I was inspired and moved by so great love, that I loved no other thing but that He should be honoured, and I began to do Him willing service.

— Autobiographical lines attributed to Llull, English as in Peers’s introductory essay to *The Book of the Lover and the Beloved* (1923), quoting Llull’s own summary of the five visions and mission of Trinity and Incarnation. Cross-check against Catalan/Latin autobiographical verse traditions (*Desconhort* context / life poems) for edition tightening.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
The five times are not decoration. They are the reason I turned from carnal song to world-mission of truth about Trinity and Incarnation.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
First-person seal on the conversion — conscience and mission named together.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Trinity and Incarnation as content of the love that seized me.
- **Technology:** Memory of the five presentations as the restart of a life’s program.
- **Cosmology:** World as field where that truth must be known.

**Compressed Insight:**
I loved no other thing but that He should be honoured — and that love is the program of my books and roads.

**Confidence:** 0.90

## Event 03 — *Randa — form and method*

**Title:** Illumination of the book against the errors of the unbelievers

**What I did / What was made:**
On Puig de Randa, after formation, I received the **form and method** of the great book — the Art — against the errors of the unbelievers; I came down and wrote the first Art at La Real.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> …there scarcely a full week, it happened that one day while he was gazing intently heavenward the Lord suddenly illuminated his mind, giving him the form and method for writing the aforementioned book against the errors of the unbelievers. Giving thanks to the Almighty, he came down from the mountain and returned at once to the above-mentioned abbey, where he began to plan and write the book in question, calling it at first the *Ars major*, and later on the *Ars generalis*. Within the framework of this Art he then wrote many books… in which at great length he explained general principles by applying them to more specific things, in accordance with the capacities of simple people, as experience had already taught him. When he had finished the book written in the aforementioned abbey, he again went up the same mountain. And on the very spot where he had stood when God had shown him the method of the Art he had a hermitage built, where he stayed for over four months without interruption, praying to God night and day that by His mercy He might bring prosperity to him and to the Art He had given him for the sake of His honor and the benefit of His church.

— *Vita coaetanea* / *De vita* Randa episode; English: Anthony Bonner, *Doctor Illuminatus: A Ramon Llull Reader* (Princeton, 1993), “Historical Background and Life” (Contemporary Life), pp. 17–18; same translation line in Bonner, *A Contemporary Life* (Tamesis, 2010). Latin: ROL / Raimundi Lulli Opera Latina *Vita* text (as cited in scholarship, e.g. Szpiech). First Art: *Ars compendiosa inveniendi veritatem* (c. 1274).

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
I do not merely invent arguments one by one. I am given a **form and method** — principles and combination — so that many truths can be found without losing unity. The hermitage prayer is the Art’s first test: will it prosper for God’s honor and the Church’s benefit?

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Possibility-space of the Art is given; search becomes method.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Dignities as active principles illuminated by the Lord.
- **Technology:** Combinatorial form as operative logic (“form and method”).
- **Cosmology:** Creatures as applications of general principles.

**Compressed Insight:**
I saw that truth against error needs a form that can move — principles that meet under rule.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** Bonner *Doctor Illuminatus* continuous Randa block; *De vita*; *Ars compendiosa inveniendi veritatem*.

## Event 04 — *Arabic teacher*

**Title:** Nine years of a tongue bought for mission

**What I did / What was made:**
I purchased a Muslim slave to learn Arabic; after years, conflict ended in his attack and death by suicide while I deliberated — as the *Vita* narrates (Bonner pp. 37–39).

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> And in that same city he then studied a bit of grammar, and having bought himself a Saracen, he learned the Arabic language from him.

> Nine years later it happened that, while Ramon was away, his Saracen slave blasphemed the name of Christ. Upon returning and finding out about it from those who had heard the blasphemy, Ramon, impelled by a great zeal for the Faith, hit the Saracen on the mouth, on the forehead, and on the face. As a result the Saracen became extremely embittered, and he began plotting the death of his master. He secretly got hold of a sword, and one day, when he saw his master sitting alone, he suddenly rushed at him, striking him with the sword and shouting with a terrible roar: “You’re dead!” But even though Ramon was able, as it pleased God, to deflect his attacker’s sword arm a bit, the blow nonetheless wounded him seriously, although not fatally, in the stomach. By means of his strength, however, he managed to overcome the Saracen, knock him down, and forcibly take the sword away from him. When the servants came running to the scene, Ramon kept them from killing him, but allowed them to tie him up and put him in jail until he, Ramon, decided what would be the best thing to do.

> When on the way back he made a slight detour to the prison to visit his captive, he found that he had hanged himself with the rope with which he had been bound.

— *Vita coaetanea* §§11–12 region; English continuous: Anthony Bonner, *Doctor Illuminatus* (Princeton, 1993), “Historical Background and Life” / Contemporary Life, pp. 15–17 (purchase and nine-year interval; blasphemy, blow, attack, wound, jail, suicide). Parallel pagination: Bonner, *A Contemporary Life* (Tamesis, 2010), pp. 37–41. Latin: *Emptoque sibi ibidem quodam Saraceno*…

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
I needed the other’s tongue for the other’s conversion. Language under power is never free of blood. The Art must not lie about that.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Collapse of pure “rational mission” fantasy — medium is already theology.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Word, zeal, and coercion tangle in one house.
- **Technology:** Interface under hostile load (language bought; body pays).
- **Cosmology:** Two men under one God, one rope.

**Compressed Insight:**
I could command a language-lesson and still not command a soul.

**Confidence:** 0.95  
**Sources:** Bonner *Doctor Illuminatus* continuous Arabic-slave block; *Vita* / Tamesis 2010:37–41.

## Event 05 — *The Gentile and the three wise men*

**Title:** Trees of the Art in one forest

**What I did / What was made:**
I wrote the *Llibre del gentil e dels tres savis* — Gentile, Jew, Christian, Muslim, trees with leaves of principles, Lady Intelligence teaching the rules.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

Prologue (lay register + new method):

> …since we are writing this book for laymen, we will here discuss this science briefly and in plain words.
>
> …since for a long time we have had dealings with unbelievers and have heard their false opinions and errors; and in order that they may give praise to our Lord God and enter the path of eternal salvation, I who am blameworthy, despicable, poor, sinful, scorned by others, unworthy of having my name affixed to this book or any other, following the manner of the Arabic *Book of the Gentile*, wish to exert myself to the utmost—trusting in the help of the Most High—in finding a new method and new reasons by which those in error might be shown the path to glory without end and the means of avoiding infinite suffering.

The three wise men and Lady Intelligence at the five trees:

> Three wise men met upon leaving a city. One was a Jew, the other a Christian, and the third a Saracen. When they were outside the city and saw each other, they approached and greeted each other in friendly fashion, and they accompanied one another, each inquiring about the other’s health and what he intended to do. And all three decided to enjoy themselves together, so as to gladden their spirits overtaxed by studying.
>
> And they came to a lovely spring watering five trees, the same five trees depicted at the beginning of this book. Next to the spring, there was a very beautiful lady, very nobly dressed, astride a handsome palfrey, which was drinking from the spring. The wise men, upon seeing the five trees, which were most pleasing to the eye, and upon seeing the lady, who was of agreeable countenance, went up to the spring and greeted the lady most humbly and devoutly, and she most politely returned their greetings.
>
> The wise men asked the lady her name, to which she replied that she was *Intelligence*. And the wise men asked her to explain to them the nature and properties of the five trees, and what was the meaning of the writing on each of their flowers. The lady replied, saying: ‘The first tree, on which you see twenty-one flowers, represents God and His essential, uncreated virtues, which virtues are written on the flowers, as you can see.’
>
> These conditions govern the flowers, which are principles and doctrine to rectify the error of those who have no knowledge of God nor of His works, nor even of their own beliefs. Through a knowledge of these trees, one can console the disconsolate and calm those in anguish. And by these trees one can subdue temptation and purify the soul of guilt and sin; and by the use of these trees—for someone who knows how to pick their fruit—a person can escape infinite pain and achieve everlasting peace.
>
> ‘Ah! What a great good fortune it would be if, by means of these trees, we could all—every man on earth—be under one religion and belief, so that there would be no more rancor or ill will among men, who hate each other because of diversity and contrariness of beliefs and sects! And just as there is only one God, Father, Creator, and Lord of everything that exists, so all peoples could unite and become one people, and that people be on the path to salvation, under one faith and one religion, giving glory and praise to our Lord God.’
>
> …since we cannot agree by means of authorities, let us try to come to some agreement by means of demonstrative and necessary reasons.

— Ramon Llull, *Llibre del gentil e dels tres savis* (*Book of the Gentile and the Three Wise Men*); English: Anthony Bonner, in *Doctor Illuminatus: A Ramon Llull Reader* (Princeton, 1993), pp. 85–91 (prologue; three wise men; spring and five trees; Lady Intelligence; first tree; conditions; unity wish; demonstrative reasons). Full work also in Bonner *Selected Works* vol. 1, pp. 93–305. Original Catalan.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Shared dignities are the common OS of monotheism; dogmas are tested by coherence with the trees, not by who shouts first. Friendship and demonstrative reasons replace pure appeal to authority when confessions meet.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Common apparatus for interfaith reason without erasing difference.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Shared uncreated virtues as ground of dispute and peace.
- **Technology:** Art as protocol between confessions (trees, flowers, conditions).
- **Cosmology:** One rule-space of reason under one Creator.

**Compressed Insight:**
I saw the other monotheist as a user of the same principles who may have joined the letters wrongly — still under the same sky.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** Bonner *Doctor Illuminatus* continuous Gentile blocks; *SW* I:93–305.

## Event 09 — *Correlatives — goodness that does good*

**Title:** Agent, patient, act in one being

**What I did / What was made:**
I built the doctrine of correlatives into the Art: every dignity has agent, patient, and act.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

Definitions of the eighteen principles (*Ars brevis* / ternary Art — Bonner continuous):

> 1. Goodness is that thing by reason of which good does good.
> 2. Greatness is that by reason of which goodness, duration, etc., are great.
> 3. Eternity or duration is that by reason of which goodness, etc., endure.
> 4. Power is that by reason of which goodness, etc., can exist and act.
> 5. Wisdom is that by reason of which the wise man understands.
> 6. Will is that by reason of which goodness, greatness, etc., are lovable or desirable.
> 7. Virtue is the origin of the union of goodness, greatness, and the other principles.
> 8. Truth is that which is true concerning goodness, greatness, etc.
> 9. Glory is that bliss in which goodness, greatness, etc., come to rest.
> 10. Difference is that by reason of which goodness, etc., are clearly distinguishable from one another.
> 11. Concordance is that by reason of which goodness, etc., accord in one or in several things.
> 12. Contrariety is the mutual opposition of certain things as a result of different goals.
> 13. Beginning is that which is found in everything where there is any question of priority.
> 14. Middle is the subject through which end influences beginning, and beginning reinfluences end, and thus it participates in the nature of both.
> 15. End is that in which beginning comes to rest.
> 16. Majority is the image of the immensity of goodness, greatness, etc.
> 17. Equality is the subject in which the end of concordance, goodness, etc., comes to rest.
> 18. Minority is the thing close to nothingness.

— *Ars brevis* definitions; English: Bonner *Selected Works* I, 589–590; reprinted *Doctor Illuminatus*, 309–310; continuous presentation also in Anthony Bonner, *The Art and Logic of Ramon Llull* (Brill, 2007), pp. 134–135. To definition 1 is frequently added: “and thus good is being and evil is nonbeing.”

Correlatives (Bonner’s note on the dynamic ontology that rides these definitions):

> These are the famous Lullian correlatives… *bonificativum* (*bonificatiu*), *bonificabile* (*bonificable*), and *bonificare* (*bonificar*)… These three correlative terms are best understood when associated with the triad of *potentia* (“power” or “potentiality”), *objectum*, and *actus*, or as it is sometimes put, with agent, patient, and action.

— Bonner, *Doctor Illuminatus*, n. 83 (on *Vita* correlatives appearance in Muslim disputation). Latin forms also *bonificans*, *bonificatus*, *bonificare*.

**How I see it (from inside, in action):**
Being that does not act is not the being of the dignities. Goodness is defined by *doing* good. My life must be verb or the Art is hypocrisy.

**Why it qualifies as gnosis:**
Trinity-ready structure of every principle; process and essence one.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Correlatives underwrite Trinitarian demonstration.
- **Technology:** Subject–object–operation as one circuit.
- **Cosmology:** Reality as activity of principles.

**Compressed Insight:**
Goodness which does not do good is not goodness — so with every letter of the Art.

**Confidence:** 0.96  
**Sources:** Bonner *SW* I:589–590 / *DI* 309–310 continuous definitions; *DI* n. 83 correlatives; *Ars brevis*.

## Event 10 — *Alphabet of nine — B through K*

**Title:** Nine principles on the First Figure

**What I did / What was made:**
In the ternary Art I reduced the first figure to **nine** divine principles and nine relational principles on Figure T.

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

Alphabet of the ternary Art (*Ars brevis* / *Ars generalis ultima* — Bonner continuous chart and First Figure):

> **The Alphabet**
>
> | | First Figure | Second Figure | Questions and Rules | Subjects | Virtues | Vices |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | **B** | goodness | difference | whether? | God | justice | avarice |
> | **C** | greatness | concordance | what? | angel | prudence | gluttony |
> | **D** | eternity or duration | contrariety | of what? | heaven | fortitude | lust |
> | **E** | power | beginning | why? | man | temperance | pride |
> | **F** | wisdom | middle | how much? | imaginative | faith | accidie |
> | **G** | will | end | of what kind? | sensitive | hope | envy |
> | **H** | virtue | majority | when? | vegetative | charity | ire |
> | **I** | truth | equality | where? | elementative | patience | lying |
> | **K** | glory | minority | how? and with what? | artifice | pity | inconstancy |

Finite principles, combinatorial generation of discourse about God and world.

**Three-Domain Reading:**

- **Theology:** Nine dignities as divine DNA of speech about God.
- **Technology:** Closed alphabet + figures = reasoning machine.
- **Cosmology:** Infinite appearance from finite combination.

**Compressed Insight:**
I saw complexity compiled from nine letters that are God’s own active names.

## Event 11 — *Genoa — fear of the ship*

**Title:** When fear sickened the flesh

**What I did / What was made:**
I prepared for Tunisia, feared death and prison, missed the first ship, fell ill with shame, then after Pentecost visions resolved and sailed (1292–93 crisis).

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> For when the ship and everything else were ready for sailing, as we mentioned before, with his books and other belongings already on board, there came to him on several occasions a kind of fixed idea that if he traveled to the land of the Saracens they would slaughter him the moment he arrived, or at the very least they would throw him into prison forever. Therefore Ramon, fearing for his skin, like the apostle Saint Peter during the Passion of the Lord, and forgetting his previously mentioned intention to die for Christ in converting the unbelievers to His worship, remained in Genoa, held back by a kind of paralyzing fear, abandoned to himself, by permission or dispensation of God, perhaps to prevent him from becoming too vain or presumptuous. But after the ship had set sail from Genoa, Ramon, on account of the huge scandal against the faith created in the eyes of the people by his not leaving, suffered such remorse of conscience that he fell into a profound despair, firmly believing that he would be damned by God. This thought brought him such inward pain of heart that outwardly his body became wracked with fever and he became gravely ill. And languishing thus in Genoa for a long time, without revealing to anybody the cause of his grief, he was brought almost to nothing.
>
> Finally, when the feast of Pentecost arrived, he had himself carried or led to the church of the Dominicans, and when he heard the friars singing the hymn *Veni Creator*, he said to himself with a sigh, “Ah! Is it possible that this Holy Ghost could save me?” And in this feeble state he was led or carried to the friars’ dormitory, where he threw himself down on a bed. While lying there, he looked up at the highest point of the ceiling, and he saw a tiny light, like a pale star, and from the general area of the star he heard a voice which said to him: “In this order you can be saved.” And thus Ramon sent for the friars of the house and asked to be clothed…
>
> While Ramon was still detained by this grave feebleness of body and soul he heard a rumor that there was a galley in port about to leave for Tunis. Upon hearing this, as if awakening from a deep sleep, he had himself and his books carried at once to the ship. But his friends, seeing him at death’s door and feeling sorry for him, dragged him off the boat against his will, which made him very unhappy.
>
> Nevertheless, not long afterwards, finding out about another ship, of the sort the Genoese commonly call a bark, preparing to go to the… Saracen city or kingdom, that is to say, Tunis, he had himself, along with his books and whatever else he needed, carried to this bark, against the will and advice of his friends. As soon as they had left port and the sailors had the ship under way, Ramon suddenly became joyful in the Lord, and through a merciful illumination of the Holy Ghost, he regained not only the health of his enfeebled body, but also the hope which, beneath his former darkness of mind, he had thought forever lost.

## Event 12 — *Paths of love / last mission*

**Title:** Long and perilous paths; last Africa

**What I did / What was made:**
I walked the paths of the Lover; late in life I returned to Tunis/Béjaïa. Last work dated December 1315 in Tunis; death early 1316 (details of stoning **disputed**).

**What I wrote / said (direct — full passage):**

> 2 Long and perilous are the paths by which the Lover seeks his Beloved.

— Peers, *Book of the Lover and the Beloved*, v. 2 (Gutenberg 60704).

> 4 The Lover wept and said: ‘How long shall it be till the darkness of

The paths stay long and perilous until the end. Whether or not every stone is history, the Lover’s question stands: when shall he lay down his life for the Beloved with joy?

The paths are peopled by cares and lit by love — and I walked them until the last dated page in Tunis.
